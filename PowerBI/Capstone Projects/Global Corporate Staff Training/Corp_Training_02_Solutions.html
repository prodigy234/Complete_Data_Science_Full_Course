1. Loading the clean data - Power Query solutions

Step-by-step solution for loading every one of the nine sheets from Corporate_Staff_Training_PowerBI_Dataset.xlsx and preparing them correctly in the model.

General first step: Home > Get Data > Excel Workbook > select Corporate_Staff_Training_PowerBI_Dataset.xlsx > in the Navigator, tick all nine data sheets (Dim_Date, Dim_Locations, Dim_Employees, Dim_Trainers, Dim_TrainingPrograms, Fact_Sessions, Fact_Enrollments, Fact_Feedback, Fact_Budget - skip the Read Me sheet) > Transform Data to open Power Query.
Solution 1 - Dim_Date

In Power Query, select Dim_Date, right-click the Date column header > Change Type > Date. Close & Apply, then in Model view select the table, go to the Table Tools ribbon > Mark as Date Table > choose Date as the date column, and confirm the OK prompt.

Solution 2 - Dim_Locations

In Power Query, select Dim_Locations and confirm LocationID, City, Country, Region and OfficeType are all typed as Text (the default on import). Close & Apply.

Solution 3 - Dim_Employees

In Power Query, set HireDate's type to Date via the column header type icon. Do not remove or replace blank ManagerID values - those blanks correctly represent the most senior Executives, who have no manager. Close & Apply.

Solution 4 - Dim_Trainers

Confirm TrainerID, TrainerName, TrainerType, Specialization and TrainingTypeFocus are all Text type. Close & Apply.

Solution 5 - Dim_TrainingPrograms

Set DurationHours to Decimal Number and CostPerParticipant_USD to Decimal Number via the column type icons. Close & Apply.

Solution 6 - Fact_Sessions

Set SessionDate to Date type, Capacity and ActualAttendance to Whole Number, and SessionCostUSD to Decimal Number. Close & Apply.

Solution 7 - Fact_Enrollments

Set EnrollmentDate, DueDate, CompletionDate and CertificationExpiryDate all to Date type. Do not error-check or remove blanks in Score (only Compliance quizzes have a score) or CertificationExpiryDate (only completed Compliance enrollments get one) - these blanks are expected and meaningful.

Solution 8 - Fact_Feedback

Set SubmittedDate to Date type, and SatisfactionScore, TrainerRating and ContentRelevanceScore to Whole Number type. Close & Apply.

Solution 9 - Fact_Budget

Set Month to Date type, and PlannedBudget_USD and ActualSpend_USD to Decimal Number type. Close & Apply, then load all nine queries to the model.

Final step: Click Close & Apply. Confirm in Model view that all nine tables appear with the correct icons (a calendar icon on Dim_Date once marked as a date table) and that row counts match: Dim_Date 1,461 · Dim_Locations 25 · Dim_Employees 180 · Dim_Trainers 42 · Dim_TrainingPrograms 27 · Fact_Sessions 254 · Fact_Enrollments 5,993 · Fact_Feedback 3,755 · Fact_Budget 1,056.

Go to Model view and create each relationship below by dragging from the "from" field to the "to" field, or via Manage Relationships > New. Power BI will auto-detect cardinality - always double check it matches the table below before saving.

Why the two Fact_Enrollments date relationships stay inactive: Power BI only allows one active (usable-by-default) relationship path between any two tables, because allowing more than one would make a plain filter on Dim_Date ambiguous - the engine would not know which path to propagate through. The relationship on EnrollmentDate is left active since "when someone signed up" is the most common slicing need; DueDate and CompletionDate are created as inactive (Power BI greys them out and draws them as dashed lines) and are switched on only inside specific DAX measures using USERELATIONSHIP(), which activates a named relationship for the duration of a single CALCULATE.
Why the self-referencing Employees relationship is different: Dim_Employees[EmployeeID] to Dim_Employees[ManagerID] connects the table to itself - the "one" side (a manager's EmployeeID) to the "many" side (that manager's direct reports, via their ManagerID). Because both ends sit in the same table there is no competing relationship to conflict with, so it can stay active by default. It is queried with RELATEDTABLE(Dim_Employees) from a manager's row to retrieve their direct reports.

3. DAX measures - full solutions

Every measure below is given in full, with a line-by-line explanation of what each part of the formula does, why that particular DAX pattern was chosen over the alternatives, and exactly what it drives on the finished dashboard.

Foundational measures (used across every page)

Total Employees

Total Employees =
CALCULATE(DISTINCTCOUNT(Dim_Employees[EmployeeID]), Dim_Employees[EmploymentStatus] = "Active")
Line-by-line explanation
  1. DISTINCTCOUNT counts unique EmployeeID values.
  2. CALCULATE narrows that count to only currently Active employees, so former staff never inflate headcount-based rates.
Why this pattern was used
Filtering to Active at the base measure means every measure built on top of it automatically excludes former staff, instead of every downstream measure having to repeat the filter.
Impact on the dashboard
Headcount KPI card and the denominator for several coverage-rate measures across the whole report.

Total Enrollments

Total Enrollments = COUNTROWS(Fact_Enrollments)
Line-by-line explanation
  1. COUNTROWS counts every enrollment row visible in the current filter context.
Why this pattern was used
COUNTROWS is correct (not DISTINCTCOUNT) because Fact_Enrollments is already at one-row-per-enrollment grain.
Impact on the dashboard
Base measure for completion rate and enrollment-volume visuals across the whole report.

Employees Trained (Distinct)

Employees Trained (Distinct) =
CALCULATE(DISTINCTCOUNT(Fact_Enrollments[EmployeeID]), Fact_Enrollments[Status] = "Completed")
Line-by-line explanation
  1. DISTINCTCOUNT counts each employee once even if they completed several courses.
  2. CALCULATE narrows the rows considered down to completed enrollments only.
Why this pattern was used
DISTINCTCOUNT (rather than COUNTROWS) matters here specifically because one employee can have many completed enrollments, and this measure asks 'how many people', not 'how many completions'.
Impact on the dashboard
Executive Overview KPI card - the headline reach metric for the whole training function.

Overall Completion Rate %

Overall Completion Rate % =
DIVIDE(
    CALCULATE([Total Enrollments], Fact_Enrollments[Status] = "Completed"),
    [Total Enrollments]
)
Line-by-line explanation
  1. The numerator re-uses [Total Enrollments] filtered down to Completed status.
  2. DIVIDE returns the ratio, safely returning BLANK instead of an error if there are zero enrollments in the current filter context.
Why this pattern was used
Building the numerator from the existing [Total Enrollments] measure keeps the counting logic in one place instead of duplicating COUNTROWS(Fact_Enrollments).
Impact on the dashboard
Headline KPI card on the Executive Overview page.

Total Training Hours Delivered

Total Training Hours Delivered =
CALCULATE(
    SUMX(Fact_Enrollments, RELATED(Dim_TrainingPrograms[DurationHours])),
    Fact_Enrollments[Status] = "Completed"
)
Line-by-line explanation
  1. SUMX iterates row-by-row over Fact_Enrollments.
  2. RELATED reaches across the active Dim_TrainingPrograms-to-Fact_Enrollments relationship to pull that row's DurationHours from the program catalogue.
  3. CALCULATE restricts the whole calculation to completed enrollments only, so hours are only counted once training is actually finished.
Why this pattern was used
RELATED is required (rather than a plain SUM) because DurationHours lives on the dimension table, not on Fact_Enrollments itself - SUMX plus RELATED is the standard pattern for pulling a dimension attribute into a per-row fact calculation.
Impact on the dashboard
Training-hours KPI card and trend line on the Executive Overview page.

Total Training Spend

Total Training Spend = SUM(Fact_Budget[ActualSpend_USD])
Line-by-line explanation
  1. SUM adds every ActualSpend_USD row in Fact_Budget visible in the current filter context.
Why this pattern was used
Actual spend is captured monthly by region and training type, so it must be summed from the fact table rather than estimated from the course catalogue.
Impact on the dashboard
Financial KPI cards across the Executive Overview and Budget & Cost Analysis pages.

Average Satisfaction Score

Average Satisfaction Score = AVERAGE(Fact_Feedback[SatisfactionScore])
Line-by-line explanation
  1. AVERAGE computes the mean SatisfactionScore across feedback rows visible in the current filter context.
Why this pattern was used
A plain AVERAGE is sufficient because SatisfactionScore is already a stored 1-5 value, not something computed per row first.
Impact on the dashboard
Headline experience KPI on the Executive Overview and Employee Development & Feedback pages.
Compliance training measures

Compliance Enrollments

Compliance Enrollments =
CALCULATE([Total Enrollments], Dim_TrainingPrograms[TrainingType] = "Compliance")
Line-by-line explanation
  1. CALCULATE narrows Total Enrollments down to rows whose program's TrainingType is Compliance.
Why this pattern was used
Filtering on the TrainingType column from Dim_TrainingPrograms rather than duplicating a list of program names keeps this measure correct even if the catalogue grows.
Impact on the dashboard
Denominator for Compliance Completion Rate % and the Compliance Enrollments KPI card.

Compliance Completion Rate %

Compliance Completion Rate % =
VAR CompletedCompliance =
    CALCULATE(
        [Total Enrollments],
        Dim_TrainingPrograms[TrainingType] = "Compliance",
        Fact_Enrollments[Status] = "Completed"
    )
RETURN
    DIVIDE(CompletedCompliance, [Compliance Enrollments])
Line-by-line explanation
  1. VAR CompletedCompliance layers a Status = Completed condition on top of the Compliance filter.
  2. DIVIDE expresses that as a share of all Compliance enrollments, safely handling a zero denominator.
Why this pattern was used
The two-condition CALCULATE is stored in a VAR purely for readability, since the measure is referenced again (indirectly through [Compliance Enrollments]) in the RETURN line.
Impact on the dashboard
The single most-watched number on the Compliance Training Tracking page - regulators and auditors care about this figure directly.

Overdue Compliance Trainings

Overdue Compliance Trainings =
CALCULATE(
    COUNTROWS(Fact_Enrollments),
    Dim_TrainingPrograms[TrainingType] = "Compliance",
    Fact_Enrollments[Status] <> "Completed",
    Fact_Enrollments[DueDate] < TODAY()
)
Line-by-line explanation
  1. The TrainingType filter restricts to Compliance courses.
  2. The Status filter excludes anything already completed.
  3. The DueDate filter compares against TODAY(), Power BI's current-date function, so this measure is always accurate whenever the report is opened.
Why this pattern was used
TODAY() rather than a hardcoded date keeps the measure correct on every refresh without manual edits - the same pattern used for Overdue Tasks in project-tracking dashboards.
Impact on the dashboard
The most important red-alert KPI on the Compliance Training Tracking page, and drives the 'Overdue by Employee' action table.

Certifications Expiring in 30 Days

Certifications Expiring in 30 Days =
CALCULATE(
    COUNTROWS(Fact_Enrollments),
    Fact_Enrollments[CertificationExpiryDate] >= TODAY(),
    Fact_Enrollments[CertificationExpiryDate] <= TODAY() + 30
)
Line-by-line explanation
  1. The first condition excludes certifications that have already expired.
  2. The second condition keeps only those expiring within the next 30 days, using simple date arithmetic (TODAY() + 30).
Why this pattern was used
Adding an integer to a date column in DAX returns the date that many days later, which is the simplest way to express a rolling 30-day compliance-renewal window without a separate lookup table.
Impact on the dashboard
Forward-looking KPI on the Compliance Training Tracking page - drives proactive renewal reminders before a lapse occurs.

Completions This Month (by Completion Date)

Completions This Month (by Completion Date) =
CALCULATE(
    COUNTROWS(Fact_Enrollments), Fact_Enrollments[Status] = "Completed",
    USERELATIONSHIP(Dim_Date[Date], Fact_Enrollments[CompletionDate])
)
Line-by-line explanation
  1. USERELATIONSHIP activates the otherwise-inactive Dim_Date-to-CompletionDate relationship for this calculation only, without disturbing the model's default active EnrollmentDate relationship.
  2. COUNTROWS then counts completed enrollments filtered by whichever date period is in context, following that temporarily active relationship.
Why this pattern was used
Because Dim_Date already has an active relationship to Fact_Enrollments[EnrollmentDate], reaching CompletionDate for this one measure requires explicitly switching relationships with USERELATIONSHIP rather than relying on the model's default path.
Impact on the dashboard
The line chart on the Compliance Training Tracking page showing when training actually gets finished, as opposed to when it was assigned.

Trainings Due This Period

Trainings Due This Period =
CALCULATE(
    COUNTROWS(Fact_Enrollments),
    USERELATIONSHIP(Dim_Date[Date], Fact_Enrollments[DueDate])
)
Line-by-line explanation
  1. USERELATIONSHIP activates the inactive Dim_Date-to-DueDate relationship for this one calculation.
  2. COUNTROWS counts every enrollment whose due date falls in whatever period is currently selected.
Why this pattern was used
Same pattern as Completions This Month - demonstrates that a fact table can need several different 'which date matters here' answers, each requiring its own USERELATIONSHIP call.
Impact on the dashboard
Forward-planning line/column chart on the Compliance Training Tracking page, showing upcoming compliance workload by month.
Program & session measures

Total Sessions

Total Sessions = COUNTROWS(Fact_Sessions)
Line-by-line explanation
  1. COUNTROWS counts session rows visible in the current filter context.
Why this pattern was used
Fact_Sessions is already at one-row-per-session grain, so COUNTROWS is the correct, simplest choice.
Impact on the dashboard
Delivery-volume KPI card on the Program & Session Analytics page.

Average Attendance Rate %

Average Attendance Rate % =
DIVIDE(SUM(Fact_Sessions[ActualAttendance]), SUM(Fact_Sessions[Capacity]))
Line-by-line explanation
  1. SUM totals actual attendance and total capacity separately across all sessions in context.
  2. DIVIDE expresses attendance as a percentage of capacity, safely handling any zero-capacity edge case.
Why this pattern was used
Summing attendance and capacity first and dividing once (rather than averaging a per-session percentage with AVERAGEX) correctly weights larger sessions more heavily, which is the right behaviour for a capacity-utilisation metric.
Impact on the dashboard
Utilisation KPI and gauge on the Program & Session Analytics page.

Cost Per Trainee

Cost Per Trainee =
DIVIDE(SUM(Fact_Sessions[SessionCostUSD]), SUM(Fact_Sessions[ActualAttendance]))
Line-by-line explanation
  1. SUM totals delivered session cost and total attendees separately.
  2. DIVIDE returns the blended cost per attendee, guarding against zero attendance.
Why this pattern was used
Calculated from actual delivered cost and actual attendance (not the catalogue's CostPerParticipant_USD) so it reflects what training really costs per person once no-shows and under-filled sessions are accounted for.
Impact on the dashboard
Efficiency KPI on the Program & Session Analytics page, and the y-axis of the cost-vs-attendance scatter chart.

Program Enrollment Rank

Program Enrollment Rank =
RANKX(
    ALL(Dim_TrainingPrograms[ProgramName]),
    CALCULATE([Total Enrollments])
)
Line-by-line explanation
  1. ALL(Dim_TrainingPrograms[ProgramName]) builds a table of every program name, ignoring any filter currently applied to that column, so every program is compared on a level playing field.
  2. CALCULATE([Total Enrollments]) re-evaluates the enrollment count for each program name in that unfiltered list.
  3. RANKX ranks the current program's value against all of those, 1 being the most enrollments.
Why this pattern was used
ALL is required inside RANKX so the ranking table is not limited to whatever the visual itself has already filtered down to - without it, every visible program would rank 1st against itself.
Impact on the dashboard
Powers the 'Top Programs by Enrollment' bar chart's sort order and an optional rank column in the program table on the Program & Session Analytics page.

Self-Paced Share %

Self-Paced Share % =
DIVIDE(
    CALCULATE([Total Enrollments], Dim_TrainingPrograms[DeliveryMode] = "Self-Paced"),
    [Total Enrollments]
)
Line-by-line explanation
  1. CALCULATE narrows enrollments to programs delivered Self-Paced.
  2. DIVIDE expresses that as a share of all enrollments.
Why this pattern was used
Written as a simple share-of-total rather than a full delivery-mode breakdown measure because the dashboard only needs this one modality highlighted as a callout; the full breakdown is shown directly by a chart's legend instead.
Impact on the dashboard
Callout on the Program & Session Analytics page highlighting how much training is self-service versus facilitator-led.
Budget & cost measures

Total Planned Budget

Total Planned Budget = SUM(Fact_Budget[PlannedBudget_USD])
Line-by-line explanation
  1. SUM adds PlannedBudget_USD across every budget line visible in the current filter context.
Why this pattern was used
Planned budget is captured at the same region/training-type/month grain as actual spend, so a direct SUM is correct and comparable.
Impact on the dashboard
Budget KPI cards and the Planned vs Actual chart on the Budget & Cost Analysis page.

Budget Variance

Budget Variance = [Total Planned Budget] - [Total Training Spend]
Line-by-line explanation
  1. A simple subtraction between two existing measures; positive means under budget, negative means over budget.
Why this pattern was used
Built as a measure rather than a calculated column so it recalculates correctly at every level of the report - region, training type or company-wide - as filters change.
Impact on the dashboard
Conditional-formatted KPI card and the 'variance by region' visual.

Budget Variance %

Budget Variance % = DIVIDE([Budget Variance], [Total Planned Budget])
Line-by-line explanation
  1. DIVIDE expresses the variance as a proportion of planned budget, returning BLANK rather than an error if planned budget is 0.
Why this pattern was used
A percentage allows fair comparison of variance across regions and training types of very different sizes, unlike the raw dollar variance.
Impact on the dashboard
Colour/data-label field on the Budget & Cost Analysis matrix visual.

Cost Per Employee Trained

Cost Per Employee Trained =
DIVIDE([Total Training Spend], [Employees Trained (Distinct)])
Line-by-line explanation
  1. Divides total actual spend by the distinct count of employees who completed at least one course.
  2. DIVIDE guards against a zero-trained filter context.
Why this pattern was used
Blends a financial fact table (Fact_Budget) with a training fact table (Fact_Enrollments) purely through measures, avoiding the need for a direct relationship between two tables at different, incompatible grains.
Impact on the dashboard
Efficiency KPI comparing regions/business units on the Budget & Cost Analysis page.

MoM Actual Spend Change

MoM Actual Spend Change =
VAR CurrentSpend = [Total Training Spend]
VAR PreviousSpend =
    CALCULATE(
        [Total Training Spend],
        DATEADD(Dim_Date[Date], -1, MONTH)
    )
RETURN
    DIVIDE(CurrentSpend - PreviousSpend, PreviousSpend)
Line-by-line explanation
  1. VAR CurrentSpend captures actual spend for whichever month is currently in context.
  2. DATEADD(Dim_Date[Date], -1, MONTH) shifts the date filter back exactly one month so VAR PreviousSpend recalculates [Total Training Spend] for the prior month.
  3. DIVIDE returns the percentage change, safe against a zero prior-month spend.
Why this pattern was used
DATEADD is a time-intelligence function that only works correctly because Dim_Date is marked as a proper, contiguous Date Table - this measure is used specifically to demonstrate why that modelling step matters.
Impact on the dashboard
Month-over-month trend arrow on the Budget & Cost Analysis KPI strip.
Employee development & feedback measures

Average Trainer Rating

Average Trainer Rating = AVERAGE(Fact_Feedback[TrainerRating])
Line-by-line explanation
  1. AVERAGE computes the mean TrainerRating across feedback rows in the current filter context.
Why this pattern was used
Kept separate from Average Satisfaction Score so trainer performance can be tracked and compared independently of overall course satisfaction.
Impact on the dashboard
Trainer-performance KPI and the trainer-comparison table on the Employee Development & Feedback page.

Would Recommend %

Would Recommend % =
VAR Recommenders =
    CALCULATE(COUNTROWS(Fact_Feedback), Fact_Feedback[WouldRecommend] = "Yes")
VAR TotalResponses = COUNTROWS(Fact_Feedback)
RETURN
    DIVIDE(Recommenders, TotalResponses)
Line-by-line explanation
  1. VAR Recommenders counts feedback rows where the employee said they would recommend the course.
  2. VAR TotalResponses counts every feedback row in context.
  3. DIVIDE returns the percentage, safe against zero responses.
Why this pattern was used
This is effectively a simplified Net-Promoter-style measure; VARs make the two counts explicit and easy to audit or extend later (for example, into a full NPS calculation).
Impact on the dashboard
Headline experience KPI on the Employee Development & Feedback page.

Leadership Program Participation

Leadership Program Participation =
CALCULATE([Total Enrollments], Dim_TrainingPrograms[TrainingType] = "Leadership")
Line-by-line explanation
  1. CALCULATE narrows Total Enrollments down to programs whose TrainingType is Leadership.
Why this pattern was used
Isolated as its own measure because leadership-pipeline participation is tracked and reported separately from general L&D activity in most large organisations.
Impact on the dashboard
KPI card and the 'Leadership Participation by Business Unit' bar chart.

Average Training Hours per Employee

Average Training Hours per Employee =
DIVIDE([Total Training Hours Delivered], [Total Employees])
Line-by-line explanation
  1. Divides total completed training hours by the active employee headcount.
  2. DIVIDE guards against a zero-headcount filter context.
Why this pattern was used
Expresses training investment on a per-person basis so business units of very different sizes can be compared fairly.
Impact on the dashboard
Bar chart comparing training investment per employee across business units on the Employee Development & Feedback page.

Direct Reports

Direct Reports =
COUNTROWS(RELATEDTABLE(Dim_Employees))
Line-by-line explanation
  1. RELATEDTABLE follows the self-referencing EmployeeID-to-ManagerID relationship from the current employee (acting as a manager) down to every employee row that reports to them.
  2. COUNTROWS counts how many of those direct-report rows there are.
Why this pattern was used
RELATEDTABLE is the correct function to fetch the 'many' side of a relationship from the 'one' side inside a row context - here it is what makes the self-referencing hierarchy actually usable in a measure. Multi-level (skip-level) hierarchies would extend this pattern with the PATH() and PATHITEM() functions, which flatten the whole management chain into a navigable string.
Impact on the dashboard
Manager span-of-control column in the org-hierarchy table on the Employee Development & Feedback page.
