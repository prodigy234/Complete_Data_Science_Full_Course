Task Group A - Data Preparation

Load and clean the data correctly before anything else. Getting this wrong will cause confusing errors much later, so take your time here.

  • Import all seven tables (Employees, Dim_Department, Dim_JobTitle, Dim_Location, Dim_Education, Dim_Date, Fact_PerformanceReviews) from the Excel workbook using Get Data > Excel Workbook, leaving the ReadMe and Data_Dictionary sheets out of the model.
  • In Power Query, confirm every column has the correct data type - dates as Date, IDs as Text, whole-number scores as Whole Number, and TenureYears as Decimal Number - correcting any that Power BI auto-detected incorrectly.
  • Trim and clean text columns (TrimText / CleanText where relevant) even though this dataset is already clean, so students practise the habit on a dataset that will not punish a missed step.
  • In Dim_JobTitle, add a calculated JobLevelSortOrder column (1 for Entry through 7 for Executive) and set it as the 'Sort by Column' for JobLevel, so every visual using JobLevel displays in the correct seniority order.
  • In Employees, add a calculated TenureBucket column (0-1yr, 1-3yr, 3-5yr, 5yr+) and a PromotionGapBucket column (0-1yr, 1-2yr, 2-3yr, 3yr+), either in Power Query with a conditional column or in DAX with a calculated column.
  • Disable Auto date/time in Power BI Options (File > Options and settings > Options > Data Load) before building any relationships, so Dim_Date is the only date table in the model.

Task Group C - DAX Measures To Build

Build every measure listed below. You are given the measure name and what it must calculate - work out the DAX yourself before checking the solution file. Measures are grouped in the order you should build them, because later measures in each group may depend on earlier ones.

1. Core Workforce Measures

Total Employees

This is the foundation measure almost every other workforce measure divides into or compares against. It also becomes the denominator for every percentage-based measure below.

Active Employees

Distinguishes current, working headcount from historical headcount that includes people who have since left.

Terminated Employees

The numerator for attrition rate and the base count for every 'who left and why' visual.

Average Tenure (Years)

Tenure is one of the single strongest predictors of attrition risk in real HR analytics, and a simple, honest average is the right first metric before building tenure buckets.

Average Age

Gives a quick generational profile of the workforce, useful context for succession-planning discussions.

2. Attrition & Retention Measures

Attrition Rate %

This is the single most requested HR KPI in almost every real-world Power BI HR project.

Voluntary Attrition %

Voluntary and involuntary attrition need very different management responses, so leadership almost always wants them split apart rather than blended into one number.

Involuntary Attrition %

Completes the voluntary/involuntary split so the two measures always sum to the overall Attrition Rate %, which is another useful in-class validation exercise.

Attrition Rate % (by termination date, time intelligence)

This is the measure that actually lets a line chart show attrition rate changing month by month or year by year - without it, every period would show the same flat overall Attrition Rate %, because the default relationship in this model is on HireDate, not TerminationDate.

Active Headcount (Cumulative)

A simple COUNTROWS filtered by month would only show people hired in that specific month, not the running total of everyone employed - this is one of the most common beginner mistakes in HR headcount reporting, so it is included deliberately as a teaching measure.

Average Tenure at Exit

Distinguishes 'people who barely started and left quickly' from 'people who left after a long, healthy career', which need very different retention responses.

3. Compensation Measures

Average Salary

The baseline compensation benchmark for every other pay comparison in the workbook.

Median Salary

Salary distributions are typically right-skewed by a small number of very highly paid executives, so the median is a more honest 'typical employee' figure than the average, and comparing the two teaches students why both matter.

Total Payroll Cost

Finance and executive stakeholders think in total cost, not averages, so this measure translates the same underlying column into a budgeting-relevant figure.

Budget Utilisation %

Shows what percentage of each department's allocated budget is actually being consumed by salaries, a very common real-world Finance-and-HR crossover metric.

Gender Pay Gap %

Pay equity is one of the most requested analyses in modern HR analytics and a natural showcase for variables used to compare two differently-filtered versions of the same base measure.

4. Performance & Engagement Measures

Average Performance Rating

Gives a current-state performance benchmark without needing to touch the review history fact table.

Average Engagement Score

Engagement is consistently one of the top three metrics HR leadership tracks, alongside attrition and headcount.

High Performer %

Talent-density is a key input to succession planning and retention prioritisation - you protect your high performers first.

Average Review Rating (History)

Lets students directly compare a 'current snapshot' measure against a 'full history' measure built from a proper fact table, reinforcing why the fact table exists at all.

Promotion Recommended Rate

A leading indicator of internal mobility and career progression health, useful alongside YearsSinceLastPromotion.

Average Training Hours

Connects development investment to the performance and engagement metrics sitting next to it, inviting a natural correlation discussion in class.

5. Talent & Workforce Planning Measures

New Hires (Period)

The direct count of people who joined in a given month, quarter or year - the mirror image of the attrition trend measure.

Net Headcount Change

A single number that tells leadership whether the organisation grew or shrank in a given period, combining both relationships in one measure.

Average Years Since Last Promotion

A simple stagnation-risk indicator - long gaps since the last promotion correlate strongly with voluntary attrition in real organisations.

Remote Work %

Work-model mix is now a standard, expected metric on any modern workforce-planning dashboard.

Average Span of Control

Span of control (average team size per manager) is a classic organisational-design metric, and this measure is deliberately included as the most advanced DAX pattern in the course - it introduces table functions, virtual columns and iterators together.

  • Build every measure listed in the DAX section of this document, in the five groups given (Core Workforce, Attrition & Retention, Compensation, Performance & Engagement, Talent & Workforce Planning).
  • Format every percentage measure as a percentage with one decimal place, every currency measure with a $ symbol and thousands separator, and every count measure as a whole number.
  • Write a one-line description for each measure in the Measure tools > Description field in Power BI Desktop, matching the 'why' explanation given for that measure in this document.
  • Validate the Voluntary Attrition % and Involuntary Attrition % measures sum to Attrition Rate % for at least three different department filters, as a correctness check.
